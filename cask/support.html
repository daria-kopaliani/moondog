What Cask does

Cask answers the sulfite question with your wine's own pH. Enter the measured pH and it gives you the free SO₂ that wine needs to hold the target molecular SO₂, in grams of potassium metabisulfite and in Campden tablets, with the legal ceiling shown. Cask Pro adds a batch log, fermentation math and a cellar guide.

Common questions

Why does pH matter so much? Because molecular SO₂ — the fraction that actually protects the wine — depends on pH, not on the dose. The same addition that protects a wine at pH 3.2 leaves one at pH 3.8 badly short. Cask reproduces the published molecular-SO₂ relationship and its bands.

Why won't it predict free SO₂ from a dose? Because sulfite binds, by an amount that depends on the fruit, the fermentation and the wine's history. Predicting the result of an addition without measuring it would be a guess dressed as arithmetic. Add, wait, and measure.

Why does the one-tablet-per-gallon rule get a verdict? Because it is a volume rule applied to a pH problem. Cask shows you where that habit lands at your measured pH — sometimes short, sometimes over.

Important

Cask is an educational reference of published figures — not a food-safety authority. Sulfite ceilings and permitted additives vary by jurisdiction, and sulfite sensitivity is a real medical matter for the people drinking what you make. Label accordingly.
