How to can tomato sauce (acidified): time by the book

Updated 20264 min read

Tomato sauce (acidified) is high-acid, so a boiling-water bath is safe — here are the USDA processing times and how time changes with elevation.

How long do you can tomato sauce (acidified)?

USDA processes tomato sauce (acidified) for 35 minutes for pints and 40 minutes for quarts, at a full rolling boil, jars covered by at least an inch of water. Use USDA-tested recipe only — extra vegetables (peppers, onions, etc.) invalidate the safety calculation.

How much acid per jar (the step you can't skip)

Modern tomato varieties can sit right at the pH 4.6 safety line, so USDA requires adding acid to every jar before filling:

  • Bottled lemon juice: 1 tablespoon per pint, 2 tablespoons per quart. Bottled, not fresh — bottled juice has a standardized acidity; fresh lemons vary.
  • Citric acid: ¼ teaspoon per pint, ½ teaspoon per quart.
  • Vinegar (5% acidity): 2 tablespoons per pint, 4 tablespoons per quart — safe, but NCHFP notes it can cause undesirable flavor changes, so lemon juice or citric acid is the usual pick.

If the sauce tastes too sharp, you may add sugar to balance it — but the acid itself is never optional, whichever canning method you use.

How many tomatoes you need

Sauce cooks down a lot. NCHFP figures an average of 35 pounds of tomatoes per 7-quart canner load for thin sauce (about 5 lb per quart) and 46 pounds for thick sauce (about 6½ lb per quart). If your load looks short, that's normal — simmering to a thick sauce roughly halves the volume of prepared juice.

Altitude adjustment

The 35/40-minute times hold from 0–1,000 ft. Above that, the water-bath times step up in 5-minute increments: pints 40 min at 1,001–3,000 ft, 45 min at 3,001–6,000 ft, 50 min above 6,000 ft — quarts run 5 minutes longer at each band (45/50/55). Boiling water gets cooler as elevation rises, so the jars need longer at temperature.

The pressure-canner alternative

Tomato sauce doesn't require a pressure canner, but USDA publishes tested pressure options if you'd rather run one: with a dial-gauge canner, 20 minutes at 6 lb or 15 minutes at 11 lb (0–2,000 ft); with a weighted-gauge canner, 20 minutes at 5 lb or 15 minutes at 10 lb (0–1,000 ft, one step up above that). Same acidification either way — pressure canning is a time-saver here, not a substitute for the acid. Not sure which method suits you? See water bath vs. pressure canning.

Why tomato sauce (acidified) is water-bath canned

Tomato sauce (acidified) is high-acid (pH 4.6 or below), once the recipe's added acid is included, so a boiling-water bath reaches a safe temperature. Skipping the acidification step is the classic deadly mistake — do not omit it. Leave the headspace your tested recipe lists and check every lid sealed before storing.

FAQ

How long do you can tomato sauce (acidified)?

USDA processes tomato sauce (acidified) for 35 minutes for pints and 40 minutes for quarts, at a full boil, adjusting time for altitude.

Do you water bath or pressure can tomato sauce (acidified)?

Tomato sauce (acidified) is high-acid, so a boiling-water bath is safe as long as the recipe's added acid is included.

How do you adjust tomato sauce (acidified) for altitude?

At altitude a boiling-water bath needs more time: add 5 minutes at 1,001–3,000 ft, 10 minutes at 3,001–6,000 ft, 15 minutes at 6,001–8,000 ft and 20 minutes above 8,000 ft.

How much lemon juice do you add to canned tomato sauce?

USDA requires 2 tablespoons of bottled lemon juice per quart (1 tablespoon per pint), or ½ teaspoon citric acid per quart (¼ teaspoon per pint), added to each jar before filling.

How many pounds of tomatoes make a quart of sauce?

About 5 pounds per quart for thin sauce (35 lb per 7-quart load) and about 6½ pounds per quart for thick sauce (46 lb per load), per NCHFP.

Can you pressure can tomato sauce instead?

Yes — USDA lists tested pressure options (e.g., 20 minutes at 6 lb dial-gauge, 0–2,000 ft). The jars still need the same added acid; pressure canning doesn't replace acidification.
