Baste: Quilt Backing & Binding — Support

What Baste does

Baste answers the two questions a quilter faces before buying fabric: how many yards of backing a finished top needs, and how many yards of binding. It costs a pieced backing in both seam directions, names the cheaper one, and shows what the disputed overage figure costs in fabric. Baste Pro adds a project log, packaged batting sizes and a per-quilt shopping list.

Common questions

How much bigger than the top should the backing be? Nobody agrees, and Baste shows you that rather than hiding it. The Crafty Quilter publishes 2–3" per side for a home sewing machine and 3–4" for a longarm; Trace Creek Quilting asks for 3–4" on all four sides; Nebraska Quilt Company asks for 5" per side. The one that matters is whatever the person quilting your quilt asks for, so ask them — Baste tells you what each answer costs.

Is "8 inches" the same as 4 inches per side? Yes, and that is exactly where the confusion lives. "8 inches of overage" means the total added to a dimension: 4" at each edge. Read it as 8" per side and you buy for a backing eight inches bigger each way than you need. Baste always states both forms so the two cannot be mistaken for each other.

Why does the seam direction change the yardage? A backing wider than a bolt has to be pieced from several widths, and those widths can run down the quilt or across it. Which uses less fabric depends on the quilt's proportions and flips as the quilt gets bigger — a 60" × 80" throw is cheaper seamed vertically, a 90" × 100" quilt is cheaper horizontally. Baste costs both every time.

Why does it ask for my fabric width instead of assuming 44"? Because 44" is the bolt, not what you can use. Once the selvages come off, quilting cotton yields roughly 40–42". Calculators that divide by 43" can tell you to buy one width fewer than you actually need.

Why won't it just tell me the right overage? Because the right overage is not a fact about quilting — it is a fact about the frame your quilt gets loaded onto and the quilter loading it. Baste will not invent a number that is really someone else's preference.

Are the batting sizes universal? No. The packaged sizes in the app are Hobbs' published list for Heirloom Premium 80/20, and the app says so. Other brands cut different sizes; check the package you are buying.

Important

Baste is an educational reference and a personal log — not a substitute for the guidance of whoever will quilt your quilt. It carries figures published by named third parties and does the arithmetic; it does not set standards. Published advice differs between sources and changes over time, so where your longarm quilter's requirement disagrees with the app, follow your quilter.
