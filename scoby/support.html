Frequently asked

What's free and what's in Scoby Pro?

Free forever: the pH Check, Batch Calc, and Brew Safe — the pH gate decides whether a batch is safe, so it is never behind the paywall. Scoby Pro ($6.99, one-time) unlocks the Batch Log, Second Ferment, and the Brew Guide. No subscription.

What pH should my kombucha reach?

Per the Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture's brewing and bottling guidelines, the food-safety threshold is pH 4.2. Kombucha starts near pH 5. If the reading is still above 4.3, keep fermenting and re-measure. Below 2.5 is unsafe to drink as-is — it can be diluted with fresh brewed tea or distilled water until it is above 2.5, but never back above 4.2.

My batch is on day seven and still above 4.2. Now what?

Per the same guidance: discard all of it and start a new batch, preferably with a newly purchased commercial culture. This is the rule Scoby exists to surface — a ferment that slow usually means a contaminated culture or a vessel kept too cold, and "leave it another week" is not the published answer.

There's something growing on top — is that mold?

A new pellicle forms on the surface every batch and can look bumpy, pale, or stringy-brown underneath — that is the culture. Mold is dry, fuzzy, and sits on the surface in blue, green, black, or white spots. Per the guidance: discard all kombucha showing signs of mold contamination, and never reuse that culture for inoculum.

Why doesn't the second-ferment screen calculate bottle pressure?

Because no published model exists for home kombucha bottles, and a made-up number would be a number attached to something that fails as flying glass. The screen gives the controls that actually work: burp daily, and refrigerate to stop the ferment.

Do I need a pH meter, or will strips do?

The guidance names a calibrated digital pH meter as the monitoring method. Strips can get you in the neighbourhood, but the difference between 4.2 and 4.4 is the entire safety decision, and strips are not reliable at that resolution.
