Pour: Epoxy Resin Calculator — Support

What Pour does

A two-part epoxy publishes two mix ratios — one for measuring by volume, one for measuring by weight — and they are often different numbers. Pour asks how you are measuring, then splits your total into resin and hardener using the correct one. Pour Pro adds mold volume for the shapes people cast, tabletop coverage with kit counts, project profiles and a pour log.

Common questions

Why are there two ratios at all? Because resin and hardener do not weigh the same per unit volume. Equal volumes are not equal weights, so the proportion that is correct on a scale is not the proportion that is correct in cups. Every manufacturer publishes both figures for this reason.

My epoxy is 5:1. Does this affect me? Maybe not — and Pour will tell you so. WEST SYSTEM 105 with the 205 or 206 hardener is 5:1 measured either way, and the app says plainly that there is nothing to get wrong. It is the same resin with the 207 or 209 hardener that shifts to 3:1 by volume and 3.7:1 or 3.6:1 by weight.

I mixed the wrong ratio. Can I fix it? No. Epoxy is a stoichiometric reaction — both parts are consumed in fixed proportion — so neither adding more hardener afterwards nor waiting longer will cure an incorrectly mixed batch. Too much hardener leaves a piece just as soft as too little. The pour has to come out and be done again.

Why won't it let me pour my full depth at once? Epoxy makes its own heat as it cures, and depth traps it. Past a product's per-layer limit you get yellowing, cracking, shrinkage or smoke. Pour returns a layer plan rather than one number, because one number there would be advice that ruins the piece.

My product isn't in the list. Choose "My own product" and enter the two ratios from your container or its data sheet. If your manufacturer publishes only one ratio, enter it in both fields and use it in the mode it was published for.

How do I work out the volume of a heart or a figurine mold? Fill the empty mold with water and pour it into a measuring jug. That is exact. Pour deliberately does not offer a formula for irregular shapes, because a fill factor would be a guess presented as a measurement.

Does this cover UV resin? No. Pour is for two-part epoxy. UV resin is single-part and cures under a lamp, so it has no ratio to get wrong.

Important

Pour is an educational reference and a personal log — not a substitute for the manufacturer's own instructions. It carries figures published by the products it lists and does the arithmetic; it does not set specifications. Formulations differ between products and change over time, so where the label on your container disagrees with the app, follow the label.
