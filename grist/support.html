Grist: Yarn Substitution — Support

What Grist does

Grist answers the question a knitter faces standing in the shop: will this yarn stand in for the one the pattern calls for, and how much of it do I buy? It matches yarns on grist — the yards-per-weight printed on every ball band — rather than on the weight category, and it converts the pattern's ball count through yardage before turning it back into balls of the yarn you are actually buying. Grist Pro adds the buying calculator, a stash sorted by grist, and a project record.

Common questions

What is grist? Yards per unit weight. Handwoven defines it as "not a measurement of thickness, per se, but a measure of yards per pound". Your ball band already gives it to you: a ball's length and what that ball weighs. Grist normalises everything to yards per 100 g so two yarns can be compared directly.

Why not just match the weight category? Because the category is a lossy summary and the published charts do not agree on where its boundaries sit. A yarn measured at 20 wraps per inch is Fingering on Purple Lamb Fiber Arts' chart and Sport on Craftsy's — a full category apart. Their fine-end ranges do not overlap at all. Two yarns can also both be correctly labelled DK and still run 230 and 300 yards per 100 g, which is 30% apart in how far the yarn goes.

Why won't it give me one answer for wraps per inch? Because there isn't one. Grist shows you what each published chart says and names three more — The Woolery, The Crafty Jackalope and Paper Moon Knits — that differ again. If you can weigh the ball and measure its length, do that instead: grist is exact, and the categories are a summary of it.

I read that wraps per inch squared gives the yards per pound. That claim is repeated widely and it does not hold for knitting yarns. Checked against the one source that publishes both a wraps-per-inch range and a yardage band for each category, it is off by three to sixteen times, and the error grows steadily as the yarn gets heavier, so no single correction rescues it. Grist does not estimate grist from wraps per inch, and never will.

Why does it change the number of balls? Because yardage is what carries across a substitution and ball count is not. A pattern asking for ten balls of a 137 yd / 50 g yarn needs 1,370 yards; in a 231 yd / 100 g yarn that is six balls, not ten. Buying the same number of balls is the most common way to run short — or to buy far too much.

Does a good grist match mean I can skip the swatch? No. Grist tells you the yarn will go as far and sit at a similar weight; it cannot tell you how the fibre, ply and twist will behave. Swatch anyway.

Important

Grist is an educational reference and a personal log — not a substitute for knitting a gauge swatch. It carries figures published by named third parties and does the arithmetic; it does not set standards. Published guidance differs between sources and changes over time, which is why the app shows the disagreement instead of resolving it.
