Substrate hydration and field capacity

Get the water content right and colonization follows; get it wrong and contamination wins.

Do the math instantly: the mushroom substrate calculator tells you exactly how much water to add for your target moisture, and predicts yield from biological efficiency — free.

Mushroom mycelium needs moisture to colonize a substrate, but there's a narrow band that works. Too dry and the mycelium stalls; too wet and oxygen is pushed out, inviting bacteria and mold. The target is field capacity — the substrate holds all the water it can against gravity, with no free water pooling.

The squeeze test

The classic check, used across the cultivation literature: grab a handful of hydrated substrate and squeeze it hard.

What you seeVerdict
A steady stream runs outToo wet — drain and dry back
A few drops are forced outAbout right — field capacity
No water, substrate springs apartToo dry — add water

Target moisture by substrate

Different substrates hold water differently, but most fruiting substrates land around 60–65% moisture content by weight:

SubstrateTypical target
Hardwood sawdust / pellets~60%
Straw~70%
Supplemented masters mix~60–62%

Moisture content is the weight of water as a fraction of the total wet weight. If you weigh substrate before and after fully drying a sample, you can calculate it precisely rather than guessing — see how to calculate substrate moisture content by weight.

How much water to add to mushroom substrate

To hit a target moisture, add water equal to the dry weight × m ÷ (1 − m), where m is the target fraction. So 400 g of dry substrate to 60% moisture needs 600 g (≈600 mL) of water; to 65% it needs about 743 g. Subtract any water the substrate already holds. Then confirm by feel — the squeeze test above is the final check, because substrates absorb differently and heat treatment shifts moisture. For a pre-hydrated bulk like coco coir, the volumes differ; see coco coir substrate hydration.

Over-wet is the more common — and more dangerous — error.

Free water in the bottom of a bag or tub goes anaerobic and breeds bacteria that outcompete the mycelium. When in doubt, err slightly drier; you can mist a colonized block, but you can't easily un-soak a contaminated one.

Hydrate before pasteurizing/sterilizing, then check again. Heat treatment and cooling can change moisture. Confirm the squeeze test after the substrate has cooled, not just when you mixed it.

FAQ

How much water do I add to mushroom substrate?
Enough to reach field capacity — a hard squeeze forces out only a few drops. By weight that's ~60–65% moisture; add dry weight × m ÷ (1 − m) grams of water (400 g dry to 60% = 600 g water).

What is field capacity?
The point where the substrate holds all the water it can against gravity with no free water pooling — a hard squeeze gives only a few drops.

What moisture percentage should substrate be?
About 60–65% by weight for most fruiting substrates; straw nearer 70%. Err drier when unsure — over-wet breeds contamination.
