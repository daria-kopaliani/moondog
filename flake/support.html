What Flake does

Flake turns two tape measurements into a body weight, and a body weight into the day's hay in pounds. It shows both published weight formulas — Kentucky Equine Research divides by 330, University of Tennessee Extension by 300 — because for a mature horse they land 10% apart, and pretending otherwise would be the first error in the chain. Flake Pro adds a ration builder, a herd log and a condition plan.

Common questions

Why is the weight a range? Because the published formulas disagree. Both are named on screen with their own figure. A livestock scale is the only method that is not an estimate; Auburn University measured the tape-and-formula approach at about 38 lb of average error across 145 horses.

Why does it ask what my flake weighs? A flake is a unit of volume. Penn State weighed three flakes from one bale at 4.57, 3.21 and 4.54 lb; five flakes a day off a 5 lb cutting is 25 lb of hay, and off a 7 lb cutting it is 35 lb. Weigh a few flakes from a few bales with a hanging scale and average them. Flake will not guess for you.

Why won't it tell me my hay's sugar? Because it cannot. Oregon State's guidance for horses needing a low-sugar diet is forage under 10% nonstructural carbohydrates, established by sending a core sample to a forage lab. Cutting, species, weather and time of day all move it.

Why three different starch limits? They answer three different questions — starch overload (under 2 g/kg of body weight per meal), gastric ulcer risk (under 1 g/kg) and metabolic horses (under 0.3 g/kg). Which one applies to your horse is a conversation with your vet.

Important
