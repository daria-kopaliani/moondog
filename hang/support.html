Frequently asked

What's free and what's in Hang Pro?

Free forever: the Hang Check (temp + days → the cited verdict), Hang Safe, and CWD Safe — the safety content is never behind the paywall. Hang Pro ($6.99, one-time) unlocks the Hang Log, Plan the Hang, and the Aging Guide. No subscription.

How long should I hang my deer?

Two honest answers, both cited in the app. NCHFP's guidance is to age meat at 40 °F or less for no more than 2–3 days in a clean, dry, well-ventilated space. Penn State Extension's outer limit is to cut the carcass within seven days of harvest if it was chilled rapidly — sooner in warm weather. Longer dry-aging (7–21 days at 34–38 °F) is common hunter practice, and Hang labels it exactly that: common practice, not extension guidance.

It's warmer than 40 °F in my garage — can I still hang?

The extensions say no: 40 °F is the aging ceiling per NCHFP, and 40–140 °F is the bacterial danger zone, where the FDA Food Code recommendation (via Mississippi State Extension) is fewer than 4 cumulative hours. Above 41 °F Penn State's answer is coolers packed with ice. Quarter it, ice it, or take it straight to the processor.

Do I need to age meat I'm grinding?

No — per NCHFP, if you're making sausage (or grinding generally), aging is unnecessary. Cool it to 35–40 °F and process.

What's the CWD hold in the Hang Log?

In chronic wasting disease zones, the CDC's recommendation (via Mississippi State Extension) is to test every harvested deer, and Mississippi State's handling guidance is to hold the meat refrigerated at 40 °F or below for 7–10 days while results run — freezing it if results can't arrive in time. The hold toggle in the Hang Log tracks that window for you. A positive result means no part of the carcass is recommended for consumption.

Is Hang a hunting app?

No. Hang starts after a legal harvest, at the food-handling stage — it's a meat-care reference and log, the same family as a home-canning or jerky-safety app. Nothing in it predicts, assists, or depicts hunting.
