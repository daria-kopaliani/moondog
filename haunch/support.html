What Haunch does

Haunch works out how much to feed a raw-fed dog and converts the ration's edible-bone figure into the weight of an actual bone-in cut — the step most raw feeding calculators skip. Haunch Pro adds a per-dog log with weights over time, a bone check for a day's planned cuts, and batch scaling.

Common questions

Does the 10% mean 10% bone, or 10% bony cuts? Bone. Perfectly Rawsome states that edible bone percentages refer to "the actual bone found within a RMB and does not include meat and skin". A chicken wing is 46% bone, a duck neck 50%, lamb ribs 27%. So a dog needing 50 g of edible bone needs 100 g of duck necks or 109 g of chicken wings — not 50 g of either. Feeding 50 g of wings delivers 23 g of bone, under half the target.

Why does it give me a range instead of one number? Because every published source gives a range: 2–3% of ideal body weight for adults, and 8–10%, 6–8% and 4–6% for puppies as they grow. Where inside that range a particular dog belongs depends on the dog, and Haunch will not invent that for you.

Does Haunch tell me if my dog's diet is complete and balanced? No, and it says so plainly on a screen that is free forever. Hitting the 80/10/5/5 proportions does not demonstrate that a diet meets published minimums for calcium, zinc, iodine or vitamin E. No app that only knows the weight of ingredients can show that. That assurance comes from a formulation reviewed against AAFCO or NRC by a veterinary nutritionist.

My puppy is going to be a big dog. Is more bone safer? No — the opposite risk applies. AAFCO sets a tighter maximum calcium of 1.8% dry matter for "formulas that may be fed to large size puppies (those weighing 70 pounds or greater as mature lean adults)", against 2.5% otherwise, because excess calcium during growth is a skeletal problem. Haunch cannot compute percent dry matter for a home-prepared diet, so it flags that the ceiling applies to your dog rather than pretending to check it. Have that diet formulated with a vet.

Can I feed cooked bone? Never. Cooking makes bone brittle and brittle bone splinters. Every figure in Haunch assumes raw bone.

Are the bone percentages exact for my supplier's cuts? No. They are published averages, and bone content varies within a cut and between suppliers. Weigh what you actually feed, and ask your supplier whether they publish figures.

Important

Haunch is an educational reference and a personal log — not veterinary advice. It carries figures published by named third parties and does the arithmetic; it does not set standards and it does not diagnose. A dog that is unwell, losing weight or straining needs a veterinarian, not a calculator, and a diet for a puppy, a pregnant dog or a dog with kidney, liver or pancreatic disease should be formulated with one.
